A grain cellar with a fire in it

How a storeroom from 1888 became a kitchen, and where the food comes from.

The room was built to stay cold

A grain merchant had the cellar cut into the bedrock in 1888. Granite walls a metre thick, three brick vaults, one hatch to the street. Through the war it stored potatoes for the block. By the 1990s it held bicycles.

When we opened it up in 2023 we kept everything that was there and added one thing: a steel firebox where the hatch had been, with a flue that runs up the old shaft.

One fire, four ways of cooking

The fire is lit at three in the afternoon and burns birch until close. Fish and steaks go over the flame. Roots and lamb sit in the embers for hours. Herring and cheese take the smoke. A few things never meet it at all, because raw is sometimes the best way.

There is no gas line in the kitchen. When the fire is low, the menu waits.

Where it comes from

Most of what we cook is caught or grown within a day’s drive. We buy whole animals and whole fish, so the menu follows what each one gives.

  • Lake SaimaaVendace and pike-perch, by net
  • The KvarkenWhitefish and Baltic herring
  • ÅlandLamb, grazed on the outer islands
  • RuovesiGrass-fed beef
  • SipooRoots, beets and apples
